The four questions

First, a correction about the brief

The file this review was asked to verify — Sage300_to_SMEAssist_Historical_Context_for_Verification.md — does not exist anywhere on this machine, and no trace of it appears in git history. Rather than invent its contents, this review verifies the artifact that genuinely matches the description: the untracked migration-analysis/ package (25 documents, 12,426 lines, written 5–7 Sep 2026 by a prior session), whose FINAL-REPORT.md is a Phase 0 audit that ends by naming the eight of its own claims most likely to be wrong. Those became this review's attack list.

What is the real problem we are trying to solve?

It is a completeness and scope problem wearing the costume of a correctness problem. The transformation logic — Indian GST, reverse charge, rate snapping, receipt splits — is demonstrably sound: of 9,376 posted bills, 5,769 match Sage to the paise and only three non-reverse-charge documents differ by ≥ ₹1. That is not what a broken mapping looks like.

What is actually unsolved is that the migration has moved one narrow slice of the business and nothing else. Purchase invoices are 34.2% across by count and 13.5% by value — but that single average hides the real shape: AP-direct expense bills are 72.4% done while the goods/PO stream is at 0.88%. And every other object is at exactly zero: no sales invoices, no purchase orders, no goods receipts, no deliveries, no payments, no receipts, no credit or debit notes, no opening balances, no chart of accounts, no journal vouchers. Sage's window holds roughly ₹386 crore of sales that no part of this project touches.

Layered on top are two live hazards: one armed data defect that will understate ₹38 crore the next time the pipeline runs, and one script that deletes every bill in the organisation while its docstring promises it cannot.

What did we previously think the problem was?

Two different prior framings, and they should not be conflated.

The brief's framing — implicit in the 20-phase instruction — treats the flow as unknown and undiscovered: that we must establish whether sales runs OL → OE or MM → OE, whether a shared item master is the right model, and follow Sage PDF documentation to learn the purchase process. All three premises turn out to be either already settled or factually wrong (see § Rejected).

The prior audit's framing was much closer: "the transformation logic is good; its completeness, its scope governance and its safety rails are not." That framing survives this review essentially intact.

What does the actual evidence show?

The prior audit is unusually trustworthy and it reproduces exactly. Its population figures came back to the paise on independent re-derivation — AP-direct 12,781 / ₹1,763,950,260.97 and PO 18,046 / ₹1,942,654,924.63, plus the 1,079 no-distribution documents at ₹295,107,559.76. Its foundational assumption, which it explicitly asked to have checked first, is confirmed. Its most reassuring claim survived a hostile re-test.

But its single highest-priority remedy is wrong. The audit correctly identified that the goods path posts source-currency amounts as rupees, and correctly told you to switch to the ap_amount_hc column. That column is itself corrupted on at least 174 in-window documents, because the staging extract strips trailing punctuation from invoice numbers and then joins on the damaged key — pulling amounts from a different real invoice, in one proven case from a different financial year. Applying the recommended fix as written would import wrong values.

And two of the brief's central questions dissolve on contact with the data. The MM module has 68 tables and two rows. The shared item-master question is architecturally correct but empirically almost irrelevant here: of 17,129 purchased and 2,918 sold items, exactly 26 appear in both, because Indian Designs manufactures rather than trades.

What exactly should we implement?

In this order, and not in any other:

  1. Disarm work/cleanup_pilot.py today. One careless invocation destroys 9,377 bills. Cost: minutes.
  2. Do not run goods-post until the currency fix lands — and fix it by carrying real currency and conversion rate into the fields SMEAssist already has, not by switching to ap_amount_hc.
  3. Re-extract the staging mirror on the unstripped invoice number, then prove the repair by reconciling the mirror against live APOBL per document.
  4. Move the crosswalk off one laptop into the database table that already exists for it and currently holds zero rows.
  5. Give the reconciler an independent second source so it can no longer read the same wrong column on both sides.
  6. Then, and only then, decide scope — whether sales, payments and opening balances are in or out. That is a business decision that has never been put to anyone.

03

New problems this review found

not present in the prior audit

N1 · The staging mirror corrupts AP amounts by stripping invoice-number punctuation critical new

Sage stores invoice numbers with trailing punctuation — 244/, 213,., 217.., 218,,, 250-, E/16191.. The MySQL staging mirror stores them stripped, then joins the AP obligation back on the stripped key. Where stripping makes two genuinely different Sage documents collide, ap_amount_hc is populated from the wrong one.

The proof case is unambiguous. Vendor ACCI277 has two distinct real documents:

Sage APOBL: [E/16191 ] AMTINVCHC = 124,740.00 DATEINVC = 20250303 <- different FY [E/16191.] AMTINVCHC = 514,500.00 DATEINVC = 20260326 <- in window idedat_staging.sage_bill_hdr (invhseq 222493955): inv_number_raw = [E/16191] len 7 (the trailing '.' is gone) doc_total = 514,500.00 <- correct, the in-window document ap_amount_hc = 124,740.00 <- WRONG: pulled from the 2025 document

Scale. Sage holds 187 in-window documents with trailing punctuation; the mirror retains only 37, so ~150 were silently altered. 307 in-window INR documents have doc_total ≠ ap_amount_hc (net ₹2,194,191.34); 174 in-window documents worth ₹21,459,080.41 sit in vendor+invoice groups that collide once stripped.

Why it matters more than its size. The prior audit's number-one recommended action — "fix the goods currency column (ap_amount_hc, at header and line level)" — would import these wrong values as the fix. A remedy that is applied first and trusted most is exactly where a latent corruption does the most damage.

N2 · This resolves the audit's one openly unresolved contradiction new

The audit recorded a conflict it could not settle: the extract comments claim "154 whitespace documents", a flow analyst found 8 in-window, and it concluded "Unresolved — trailing whitespace is undetectable in a char column."

It was never whitespace. In the window, 0 documents carry trailing whitespace and 187 carry trailing punctuation. The original observation was real; its diagnosis was wrong, which is why nobody could reproduce it.

SELECT COUNT(*) total, SUM(CASE WHEN RTRIM(IDINVC) LIKE '%[.,/-]' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) trailing_punct, SUM(CASE WHEN RTRIM(IDINVC) <> IDINVC THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) trailing_space FROM APOBL WHERE DATEINVC BETWEEN 20260101 AND 20260430 AND IDTRXTYPE=12; total trailing_punct trailing_space 30827 187 0

N3 · SMEAssist supports foreign currency natively; the loader deliberately disables it new

The framing of the currency defect as "pick the right column" understates the options. The bill table has currency, conversionRate, purchaseType, originCountry, incoTerms and loadingPortCode. The backend honours them — reCalculateAndValidateBillCreateDto reads getConversionRate() whenever purchaseType == INTERNATIONAL.

The loader does set purchaseType: INTERNATIONAL (post_sage_bills.py:2674) — and then hardcodes "conversionRate": 1.0, "currencyDto": {"currency": "INR"} at line 2689, with a comment explaining that naming the real currency "would contradict the bills we then post". A bill is therefore declared international and simultaneously asserted to be in rupees at parity. Zero of 10,769 bills carry a non-INR currency.

This changes the recommended fix. Rather than substituting one rupee column for another and discarding the foreign-currency fact, carry currency and fx_rate — both already in sage_bill_hdr — into fields the target already models. That preserves provenance and makes the arithmetic checkable from both sides.

N4 · The entire sales side is absent from the programme, and it is as large as the purchase side new

The audit listed missing objects as an accounting gap. Measured, it is bigger than that: Sage's window holds 7,051 sales invoices worth roughly ₹386 crore (AROBL type 1: 6,850 from Order Entry + 201 AR-direct) against ₹370.66 crore of purchases. The revenue half of the business has no migration path, no target rows, and no mention in the runbook.

Sage AROBL, 1 Jan – 30 Apr 2026, by type and source type 1 (invoice) OE 6,850 3,581,225,967.17 type 1 (invoice) AR 201 278,736,410.16 type 3 (credit) OE 104 -56,421,448.06 type 3 (credit) AR 61 -92,768,733.97 SMEAssist target, same org invoice 0 · invoiceLineItem 0 · salesOrder 0 · delivery 0 · receipt 0

N5 · 16,723 products were created for bills that never posted new

Referential integrity is clean — all 36,459 bill lines resolve to real products, zero orphans. But those lines use only 727 distinct products out of 17,450 live. The other 95.8% were created by goods-masters --all-items in advance of a goods-post phase that has since moved 128 documents.

Left alone this is inert. It stops being inert because 1,419 of them carry the 9999 placeholder HSN and none carry provenance (product.meta populated on 11 of 17,886), so there is no programmatic way to tell a placeholder from a genuine classification later. Pre-creating masters ahead of the transactions that justify them converts a recoverable gap into a permanent one.

Conventions that must be known before reading any table. Dates are YYYYMMDD integers, not date types — so the window is DATEINVC BETWEEN 20260101 AND 20260430. The suffix HC means home currency (INR) and TC means the transaction's own currency; every amount exists in both. The fiscal year runs April–March and is labelled by its ending year, so this window straddles two: Jan–Mar is FY2025-26 and April is FY2026-27, which is why the loader keeps two counter series (SAGE and SAGE27).

A latent fragility in extract.sql design risk

The extract takes headers from APOBL (posted) and lines from APIBD (batch), joined on (CNTBTCH, CNTITEM). That works only because this company has never purged posted AP batches. If it ever did, APOBL would survive and APIBD would vanish — and the extract would return zero lines for every bill, silently, since the join is an EXISTS filter rather than an error. APOBLJ is the purge-proof alternative. This is not a live bug; it is a tripwire under a future housekeeping action.

07

The purchase flow, as the data actually runs it

Told without Sage jargon first: somebody asks for goods; a buyer turns that request into an order with a supplier; the goods arrive and are counted into the warehouse — and that is the moment stock and its value change; later the supplier's invoice arrives and is matched to what was received; that invoice becomes a debt owed; the debt is written into the accounting journals; and eventually it is paid. Two details in that sentence are the ones people get wrong: the request step is real and usually present, and stock moves when goods are received, not when the invoice is booked.

Where the two streams diverge, and a trap

Every AP obligation carries SRCEAPPL, which says whether it arrived from Purchase Orders or was keyed directly into Accounts Payable. This is the split that governs the whole migration, because the loader runs two entirely separate populations against it.

SRCEAPPLMeaningRaw docsLogical invoicesValueMigrated
APKeyed straight into payables — services, expenses, overheads 12,78112,7811,763,950,260.9772.36%
POMatched to a purchase order — goods 18,04614,6281,942,654,924.630.88%

The trap: 427 AP-direct invoices carry a PO number in a field. That does not make them PO-matched — SRCEAPPL does. Selecting on the presence of a PO number instead would move 427 documents into the wrong stream, with the wrong classifier and the wrong ledger treatment.

Receipt splits. 6,421 of the 18,046 PO headers carry a *N suffix — DN-FD-082*1, *2 — because one supplier invoice was received in several deliveries. Consolidating them gives 14,628 logical vendor bills, of which 975 span 2 to 36 parts. The logical invoice is the bill, and the loader is right to consolidate. This also matters for reconciliation: comparing raw document counts across the two systems will never agree, and should not.

Sales flow — and why the brief's question was the wrong one

The brief asks which of four routes the sales flow takes. Row counts settle it before any tracing is needed: MM is empty, so two of the four candidates cannot be true. What remains is a standard Order Entry spine with two custom modules attached at either end — OL in front of it for order costing, IE behind it for export documentation.

The revenue half of the business has no migration path at all scope gap

₹386 crore of sales in the same four-month window as the ₹371 crore of purchases the project has been working on. There is no extract, no loader phase, no target row and no mention in the runbook. Whether that is a deliberate scope boundary or an omission is not recorded anywhere I can find — and that ambiguity is itself the finding.

What I did not verify on this side

I established the shape of the sales chain and its size. I did not trace an individual sales invoice through shipment to inventory, COGS and the general ledger, and I did not establish how OL order IDs join to OE documents. Since nothing on this side is being migrated, that tracing has no current consumer — but it must be done before any sales scope is committed to, and it should not be assumed to mirror the purchase side.

09

SMEAssist architecture and backend behaviour

MySQL 8.0.46, 285 tables in smeassist, of which about 39 are in use for this organisation. Tenancy is cross-schema: organisation and employee live in a separate yoda schema. As in Sage, referential integrity is essentially unenforced — 23 declared foreign keys across 285 tables, and none on bill, product, contact, voucherEntry or financeAccount. Integrity here is a property of the loader, and measured, the loader achieves it: zero orphan productIds across 36,459 bill lines.

The governing constraint: amounts cannot be carried, only reproduced confirmed in source

Every migration design decision follows from this one method. The server recomputes the bill total from the lines, compares it to what the client sent, and on mismatch warns and proceeds:

// BillServiceImpl.java ~2186 if (!BigDecimalUtils.isEqualR(totalPrice, billCreateDto.getBillAmount())) { String totalPriceWarningMessage = String.format( "Mismatch detected total price: ... | Given: %s | Calculated: %s", billCreateDto.getBillAmount(), totalPrice); googleChatService.sendErrorMessageOnGoogleChat(totalPriceWarningMessage, ...); logger.warn(BILL_MARKER, totalPriceWarningMessage); } billCreateDto.setBillAmount(totalPrice); // <-- outside the if. unconditional.

The caller receives 200 OK carrying a number it did not send. So Sage's stated total can never be transferred as a value — it can only be reconstructed, by choosing per-line quantity, unit price and tax rate such that the server's own arithmetic lands on Sage's figure. The loader's author understood this (post_sage_bills.py:1463-1474), and it is why the migration is so much harder than a data copy.

Second-order consequence, and the reason reconciliation is mandatory rather than optional: a bill can post successfully, return 200, and hold a different amount from the one intended, with the only trace being a log line and a chat webhook. Presence in the target proves nothing about correctness.

Provenance is asymmetric: bills keep it, products lose it

No create DTO exposes id, and there is no first-class external-reference column on any entity. Provenance therefore survives only where a JSON blob happens to be persisted — and that differs by entity:

bill.metadata NOT NULL on 10,769 / 10,769 {"sageDoc","sageVendor", "sageBatch","sageItem","sageRcm", "sageTaxGroup","migrationSource"} product.meta NOT NULL on 11 / 17,886

That asymmetry is what makes an independent bill-level reconciliation possible at all — and it is exactly what makes the 1,419 placeholder-HSN products unidentifiable after the fact. bill.metadata is doing the work a proper source-reference column should do, and it is doing it unindexed and unvalidated.

Other verified backend behaviours

  • Foreign currency is supported and unused. bill.currency, conversionRate, purchaseType, originCountry, incoTerms, loadingPortCode all exist, and the recalculation path honours conversionRate when purchaseType == INTERNATIONAL. Zero of 10,769 bills carry a non-INR currency.
  • GST is decided by billing-address state, not by the GSTIN. The tax split reads a pincode-derived address, so a wrong pincode books the wrong tax head.
  • @NotNull is decorative — no @Valid on the bill or contact controllers, so required-field enforcement is whatever if statement happens to exist in the service.

The eight questions, answered directly

QuestionAnswer from the data
Does the same ITEMNO appear in both purchasing and sales? Rarely. 26 of 17,129 purchased items, 26 of 2,918 sold.
Are there cases where purchase and sales use different identifiers? Yes, systematically — they are different categories of item, not different codes for the same item. This is a make-to-order manufacturer, not a reseller.
Does Sage maintain one item master? Yes. One ICITEM table, 1,196,108 rows, spanning raw material, work in progress, finished goods and scrap. One master, disjoint populations within it.
Does SMEAssist have one shared Item master? Yes. One product table, no purchase/sale distinction anywhere in its schema.
Should we reuse one SMEAssist item? Yes — and for the 26 it is the only correct answer. But this decision governs 26 records, not the migration.
Which attributes are master-level? skuCode, productName, description, hsnCode, unitOfMeasurement, categoryId, typeOfStock. Note product holds a single unitPrice, hsnCode and gstPercentage.
Which attributes are transaction-specific? Quantity, unit price, tax rate, discount and ledger — all on billLineItem. This is what makes the desired model work: because price lives on the line, one product can carry a supplier price in one context and a customer price in another without duplicating the master.
Which relationships map separately? Item→supplier and item→customer pricing, tax and account context. None of these has a master-level home in SMEAssist; all must be carried per transaction.

The real item-master risk is elsewhere reframe

The duplicate-item worry the brief raises is not this project's item problem. The actual ones are: (1) 1,419 products carry the 9999 placeholder HSN and cannot be distinguished from genuine classifications because product.meta is empty; (2) 16,723 products exist for bills that never posted; and (3) Sage's work-in-progress categories — 5WPSEW (58,724 items) and 5WPCUT (47,930) — represent manufacturing stages with no SMEAssist equivalent at all, which is a modelling gap rather than a mapping one.

Existing migration scripts — what they do before what is wrong with them

post_sage_bills.py is 3,943 lines and exposes nine phases: cleanup · masters · dryrun · post · verify · legs · goods-masters · goods-dryrun · goods-post. It runs two entirely separate populations — AP-direct and goods/PO — with separate readers, separate classifiers and separate phases, sharing one payload builder, one crosswalk and one posted.log. run_all.sh sequences four of those phases strictly one at a time, having measured that two concurrent phases throttle each other to 12 bills/min against 63 alone.

Read fairly, the design is thoughtful. It is resumable by construction (posted.log is append-only and skipped entries are genuinely skipped), it relocates the Sage host when DHCP moves it rather than running blind, and it refuses to start when a second run is live because concurrent phases corrupt the crosswalk. The preflight in run_all.sh is unusually well reasoned — it explains, in comments, the exact failure it exists to prevent and what running anyway would cost.

A structural observation about the safety model

The dangerous scripts are all in work/ — one-off repair tools that write to the live system, sitting beside read-only probes with no naming convention, no --apply convention and no separation. run_all.sh is carefully guarded; the things next to it are not guarded at all. The risk is not that any individual script is reckless, it is that a reader cannot tell which is which, and one of them lies in its docstring.

17

Reconciliation — the two blind spots that matter

A reconciliation whose two sides read the same column cannot detect that column being wrong

This is precisely how a ₹48 crore currency error stays invisible: the reconciler compares doc_total in the mirror against doc_total-derived values in the target and finds agreement, because both sides inherit the same mistake. Any check must draw its expected value from a source the loader did not use — live APOBL.AMTINVCHC, not the staging column.

Presence is not correctness

items_without_ledger: 0 and bill_lines_null_ledger: 0 were green throughout the period the prior audit says ₹8.72 crore was being misfiled. Both checks ask "is a ledger attached?" and neither asks "is it the right one?" Given the backend silently overwrites amounts, a green presence check is close to meaningless on its own.

The governing principle the current suite lacks

Every Sage document must land in exactly one of four buckets, and the four must sum to the Sage total:

posted-and-correct + posted-and-wrong + not-posted-but-loadable + deliberately-excluded = every in-window Sage document

Today the tooling conflates the last two and hides the fourth entirely, which is why the project's own two reports disagree (18,006 vs 11,179 outstanding) with nothing to explain the gap. Until the four buckets balance, "how much is left" has no defensible answer.

What a working check looks like

The check in § 02 is offered as the pattern: it joins bill.metadata to live Sage, consolidates *N parts, reports no-match separately from mismatch, and splits reverse-charge out rather than letting it pollute the tolerance. It found zero unmatched documents, reproduced the known defect exactly, and surfaced one document the prior audit had not named — which is what a reconciliation is supposed to do.

Final recommendation

Treat this as a working pilot that has proven its method on one document type, not as a migration that is two-thirds finished.

The evidence supports an unusually clear split. The hard part is done and it is right. Indian GST, reverse charge, rate snapping, receipt-split consolidation, ledger construction, double-entry balance — all of it reproduces against live Sage, and where it differs it differs by rounding or by a documented structural divergence. Three non-RCM documents out of 9,376 differ by a rupee or more, and two of those are one known bug with a known cause. That is a good result and the people who built it should be told so.

The easy parts are what is missing. Not because they are technically hard, but because they were never scoped: sales, purchase orders, receipts, payments, notes, opening balances, inventory. The programme has been reporting a percentage against a denominator that only counts purchase invoices, which makes 34.2% sound like progress toward an end state rather than progress through one of eight objects.

The single most important thing to change

Stop reporting one completeness number. AP-direct expense bills are 72.4% across; the goods stream is 0.88%; everything else is 0%. Those three facts lead to three completely different conversations, and averaging them into one produces a number that supports none of them. Every decision in the plan above follows from telling them apart.

And before anything else runs: disarm the delete script, and do not let goods-post reach the API until the currency handling is fixed — with the real currency carried, not with the column the prior audit recommended, which is itself corrupted on at least 174 documents.

Method and posture. Every statement issued against both databases was a SELECT, SHOW or DESCRIBE; no migration phase was run, not even dryrun, which reaches the live API. No backend code, script, configuration or data was modified. The one write performed was work/find_sage.py --write updating SQL_HOST in .env, because the Sage laptop had moved to a new DHCP address and was otherwise unreachable. Figures are as at 8 September 2026 against Sage IDEDAT and SMEAssist org wonderblues. Where a prior claim could not be reproduced it is marked not reproduced rather than refuted; failing to reproduce a finding is not the same as disproving it.
